# Canonical normal form for synthetic MPM — specification v3

Status: **normative for the sampler, the DSL and the evaluation suite.**
Owner: Team B (identifiability + MDL). Companion measurements: `analysis/findings.md`,
reproducible via `nice -n 15 python3 analysis/identifiability.py`.

---

## 0. Why a normal form at all

MPM is a *description* language, and the map from description to rendered performance
is massively many-to-one. Three different MPMs can render to bit-identical millisecond
onsets:

| aliasing | example |
|---|---|
| re-segmentation | one 16-beat constant ≡ four 4-beat constants at the same bpm |
| degenerate transitions | `transition.to == bpm`; `meanTempoAt <= 0` (→ constant at *target*); `meanTempoAt >= 1` (→ constant at *start*); a dangling final transition (inert) — all verified in `meico/TempoMap.java:243-265` |
| cross-map attribution | an onset shift can be written as tempo, as rubato, as articulation `absoluteDelay`, as asynchrony, or as imprecision |

Training a sequence model on an un-canonicalised sampler means training it to predict an
arbitrary element of a large equivalence class: the loss is irreducibly high, the model
learns the sampler's *tie-breaking noise*, and no instruction-space metric is meaningful.
The normal form picks **one** representative per equivalence class, and it picks it so that
the inverse problem (performance → MPM) is *well-posed*: the representative is (i) unique,
(ii) recoverable from the observable data, and (iii) minimal in description length.

The MDL statement of the goal, measured in `analysis/mdl.py`:

```
DL(piece)  = number of canonical-DSL tokens          (model cost)
L(data|M)  = Gaussian code length of the render residual at 1 ms  (fidelity cost)
canonical MPM must minimise DL at equal fidelity, i.e. dominate every rival explanation
```

Measured on 100 `val_v2` pieces (`analysis/findings.md` §A): GT canonical tempo costs a
**median 38 tokens at ≤7e-12 ms** render error; the cheapest piecewise-constant rival
reaching the same fidelity costs **3.55×** as many tokens, and a staircase held to the
*same* token budget is **131 ms** worse. Canonical MPM dominates. That dominance is the
operational meaning of "most efficient and natural representation".

**Scope caveat (important).** Those pieces were *generated by the canonical sampler*, so
§A is a self-consistency result: the generating hypothesis class necessarily wins on its
own samples. It establishes that the normal form is internally well-posed and that the
staircase rival is priced fairly — it does **not** establish that canonical MPM is the
most efficient description of a *human* performance. The queued test for that
(`LOG.md:120-124`) is the same staircase-oracle fit on the Vienna 4x22 corpus; it has not
been run. Until it has, no claim in this document should be read as being about real
performances.

## 1. Attribution hierarchy (normative)

meico's renderer fixes the order in which maps compose (`Performance.java:507-555`).
The normal form assigns each map a **disjoint band of the timing/velocity signal** so that
the composition is invertible band-by-band.

```
 render order (meico)              canonical band                       v3 status
 1  dynamicsMap        (ticks)     the velocity field: smooth,          IN
                                   band-limited, >=4-beat segments
 2  movementMap                    pedal CC64/67                        OUT (future)
 3  metricalAccentuationMap        periodic velocity accents            OUT (aliases 1 & 4)
 4  articulationMap (non-ms)       per-DATE duration/velocity           IN (Team A)
                                   deviations only
 5  rubatoMap          (ticks)     frame-periodic INTRA-frame onset     IN (Team A)
                                   redistribution, zero net displacement
 6  ornamentationMap               note-generating                      OUT (unrenderable)
 7  tempoMap           (ticks→ms)  smooth INTER-boundary timing,        IN
                                   >=4-beat segments
 8  asynchronyMap      (ms)        constant per-part offsets            OUT (future)
 9  articulationMap (ms)           ms duration/delay modifiers          OUT (aliases 5 & 8)
10  imprecisionMap     (ms)        residual distribution parameters     OUT (future)
```

**H1 — tempo owns all smooth inter-boundary timing.** Any timing signal that is smooth on
a ≥4-beat scale is tempo, never rubato and never articulation.

**H2 — rubato owns *only* frame-periodic intra-frame redistribution with zero net
displacement.** With the canonical `lateStart=0, earlyEnd=1` (§4.R2) the warp
`t ↦ t − ℓ + frame·(ℓ/frame)^intensity`, `ℓ = (t − start) mod frame`, fixes every frame
boundary **exactly, in ticks**: every multiple of `frame` is a fixed point, so the warp
adds no net displacement at the frame grid and cannot masquerade as a global tempo change.

That is a statement about the *tick* grid, and it is the strongest form of H2 that
survives measurement. It does **not** say the rubato band is orthogonal to the tempo
family in the ms-residual sense: a tempo map with boundaries *finer* than the frame can
still track part of the warp between the fixed points. Measured (findings §B): an optimal
**1-beat** staircase absorbs 55 % of the warp at `frameLength = 720`, 83 % at 1440 and
94 % at 2880. What canonical form buys is that the *canonical-density* rival (≥4-beat
segments) cannot: it leaves a 34.0 ms median residual, versus 0 for the rubato
explanation, at 3× the tokens. The separation is an MDL separation, not an orthogonality.
§7.2 states the same limit; earlier releases of this document overstated H2 here and are
superseded.

**H3 — articulation owns *only* per-date duration and velocity deviations.**
No `absoluteDelay`, no `absoluteDelayMs`, no `absoluteDurationMs*` (those are ms-domain and
would alias rubato, asynchrony and imprecision). Articulation never moves an onset.

**H4 — dynamics owns the velocity field.** Every note velocity is
`dynamicsAt(onset) + articulation.absoluteVelocityChange(onset)`, with the second term
sparse (§4.A1). `metricalAccentuationMap` is excluded from v3 precisely because a periodic
accent pattern is expressible both as accentuation and as a set of articulations — an
unresolvable alias until one of them is given a hard structural constraint.

**H5 — asynchrony and imprecision are future work.** Until they are sampled, *all* residual
between the rendered performance and the four in-scope maps is zero by construction. This
is what makes the current exactness proofs (`identifiability.py` §V) meaningful: any
non-zero residual is a bug, not noise.

## 2. Global invariants

| id | rule | why |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | `ppq = 720`, `beatLength = 0.25` (quarter) everywhere | `python/tempo_math.py` hardcodes both; beatLength re-encodings are a pure alias (they change `bpm` and `beatLength` together with no render effect) |
| G2 | one performance, one part (`"Piano"`, ch 1), all maps **global** | a part-local map that shadows a global one is an alias for having only the local one (`Performance.java:478-490`) |
| G3 | every map used has an instruction at `date = 0` | otherwise meico falls back to silent defaults (100 bpm / velocity 100) that the model would have to learn as an implicit token |
| G4 | instruction dates are **integer multiples of `ppq`** (beats) for tempo, dynamics, rubato | makes dates a small integer alphabet; sub-beat boundaries are the staircase failure mode (§5) |
| G5 | literal values only — no `styleDef` / `name.ref` indirection in v3 | one syntactic form per semantic value |
| G6 | canonical precision: `bpm`, `volume` → 1 decimal; `meanTempoAt`, `curvature`, `protraction`, `intensity`, `relativeDuration` → 2 decimals; `absoluteVelocityChange` → integer | the DSL digit-tokenises numbers, so precision *is* description length; 0.1 bpm ≈ 0.1 % ≈ sub-ms over a segment. Note this makes canonical form *lossy at the last digit* against an un-rounded rival: the exact per-onset velocity skyline reproduces the rendered velocities to 0.018 RMS, not 0 (findings §A2) |
| G7 | the last instruction of every map is **constant** (no dangling transition) | meico renders a dangling transition as inert, so `... R 90 M 0.4` and `... C` are render-identical — a pure alias |
| G8 | adjacent instructions that are semantically equal are merged | re-segmentation alias |
| G9 | per-piece deterministic seeding (`seed*1_000_003 + i`) | reproducibility of the exactness proofs |

## 3. Currently implemented (v2, `java/SampleAndRender.java`)

Read verbatim from the generator, for the record.

**Score** (`sampleScore`, `generatePiece`)
- total length `16 + U{0..32}` quarters → 16–48 beats, 4/4, single voice + chords
- durations `{180, 360, 720, 1440}` ticks with p = `{0.2, 0.4, 0.3, 0.1}`
- 8 % rests; 15 % of events are chords of 2–4 notes (offsets `+3..+11` semitones)
- pitch random walk `±7`, clamped `[36, 90]` (chord tones clamped `[30, 96]`)

**tempoMap** (`sampleTempoMap`)
- boundaries at beat multiples, step `4 + U{0..12}` → **segments 4–16 beats**
- `bpm` log-uniform `[40, 200]`, rounded to 1 decimal
- 60 % **tempo continuity**: `bpm := previous segment's end tempo`
- transition with p = 0.5, never on the last instruction
- transition depth `|log2(to/bpm)| >= 0.15` (≈ 11 %), rejection-sampled
- `meanTempoAt` uniform `[0.15, 0.85]`, 2 decimals
- adjacent constant instructions with equal bpm are dropped (G8); last is constant (G7)

**dynamicsMap** (`sampleDynamicsMap`) — mirrors tempo
- same boundary policy (4–16 beats)
- `volume` uniform `[30, 115]`, 1 decimal; 60 % continuity; transition p = 0.5
- transition depth `|to − volume| >= 8`
- `curvature` uniform `[0, 0.9]`, `protraction` uniform `[−0.7, 0.7]`, 2 decimals
- G7/G8 as above

**Emitted record**: `{"id", "ppq", "notes":[[date, dur, pitch, msOn, msOff, vel]], "tempo":[[date,bpm,to|null,mta|null]], "dynamics":[[date,vol,to|null,curv|null,prot|null]]}`.

## 4. v3 additions (Team A) — rules and their identifiability justification

### R — rubatoMap

| id | rule | why the inverse is well-posed |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | `frameLength ∈ {720, 1440, 2880}` ticks (1, 2, 4 beats), constant within a span | a small discrete alphabet turns frame estimation into classification instead of continuous search; the frame is *observable* as the period of the onset-residual autocorrelation |
| **R2** | `lateStart = 0`, `earlyEnd = 1`, always | **the central identifiability rule.** Frame boundaries become fixed points ⇒ the warp has zero net tick displacement at the frame grid (H2). With `lateStart > 0` the whole frame shifts, which is *exactly* an asynchrony/tempo offset and destroys the decomposition. It also removes a real cross-implementation divergence: mpmify's TS port clamps `lateStart` to `[0, 0.9]` and `earlyEnd` to `[0.1, 1]` (`InsertRubato.ts:19-20`); meico clamps `lateStart < 0` → 0 and `earlyEnd > 1` → 1, and additionally resets *both* to 0.0/1.0 when `lateStart >= earlyEnd` (`RubatoMap.java:197-213`, third branch at 207-211). At 0/1 both agree |
| **R3** | `intensity ∈ [0.45, 2.2]`, 2 decimals, excluding a deadband that **depends on `frameLength` and on the piece's tempo range** (table below) | `intensity = 1` is the identity warp; near it the observable displacement falls below the ms noise floor while the *token cost stays the same*, so a model trained on near-identity rubato learns to emit unfalsifiable instructions. The warp amplitude in *milliseconds* scales with the frame **duration**, so a single frame-independent deadband cannot hold for all three frame lengths — measured in findings §D |
| **R4** | `loop = "true"` on every span | meico's default is `loop = false` (`RubatoData.java:29`), under which **only the first frame is warped** and the rest of the span is silently unaffected. A span of ≥8 beats with `loop=false` is a different (and much weaker) object than the one the label claims |
| **R5** | span length ≥ 8 beats **and** an integer multiple of `frameLength`, starting on a multiple of `frameLength` from the span start | ≥2 (usually ≥4) complete frames are needed before a *periodic* explanation beats a per-note one; a partial trailing frame is a non-periodic residual that tempo would have to absorb |
| **R6** | every span is closed by a **neutral terminator**: a second `<rubato>` at the span end with `intensity=1, lateStart=0, earlyEnd=1` | `getEndDate` only scans for `rubato` elements (`RubatoMap.java:316-326`); without a terminator the warp runs to `Double.MAX_VALUE` and leaks into every later note. The terminator is the rubato analogue of G7. Its `loop` and `frameLength` are irrelevant (the warp is the identity either way); the shipped sampler writes `frameLength=720, loop=false` and that is conforming |
| **R7** | rubato spans do not overlap; at most one span active at any date | overlap has no meico semantics (the *last* instruction at or before the date wins), so overlapping spans are an alias |
| **R8** | **no tempo instruction may fall strictly inside a rubato frame**: every tempo date in `(spanStart, spanEnd)` must satisfy `(date − spanStart) mod frameLength == 0` | meico does **not** compose the two maps the way the MPM semantics suggest. `TempoMap.renderTempoToMap` picks a note's tempo segment from the note's *unwarped* `GenericMap` key (`TempoMap.java:396-404`) but evaluates the tempo formula on `date.perf`, which `RubatoMap.java:368` has already warped **without touching the key**. If a frame straddles a tempo boundary, a note can be rendered under a segment that does not contain its own performance date: with `intensity > 1` the warped date can fall *before* the segment start, and for a power transition meico then evaluates `Math.pow(negative, non-integral)` = **NaN milliseconds**. Measured on a 40-piece straddling probe: 24/2485 notes shift by up to **316 ms** relative to the naive `tempo(warp(t))` model and 4 notes render as NaN (`analysis/RubatoProbe.java` mode `multi`, proof V5–V7). The shipped sampler already enforces this in `pickFrameLength` (`java/SampleAndRender.java:272-289`); this rule makes it normative so a sampler written from the spec alone cannot omit it |

**R2 resolved (2026-08-08, Team A fix round + orchestrator):** the sampler now pins `lateStart=0`, `earlyEnd=1`, `loop=1` on every span and terminator (audited: 297/297 spans in the regenerated pilots). The compact `U..F..I..X` DSL production without S/E slots is therefore canonical; the S/E grammar extension in §5 stays documented for a future decision to reopen R2.

**R3 deadband, calibrated (findings §D).** Onset-displacement RMS in ms that survives the
best single constant tempo, and the intensity band inside which it stays under the 5 ms
observability floor this document uses elsewhere (§4.T2, findings §C):

| frameLength | warp RMS at i = 1.07, 100 bpm | deadband @100 bpm | @200 bpm | @240 bpm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 720 (1 beat) | 7.1 ms | [0.95, 1.05] | **[0.91, 1.10]** | **[0.89, 1.12]** |
| 1440 (2 beats) | 13.8 ms | [0.98, 1.02] | [0.95, 1.05] | [0.94, 1.06] |
| 2880 (4 beats) | 27.6 ms | [0.99, 1.01] | [0.98, 1.02] | [0.97, 1.03] |

(2-decimal roundings of the exact bisection; raw edges in `analysis/out/results.json`
under `rubato_observability.deadband_5ms`. Round **outward** when implementing.)

The shipped `[0.95, 1.05]` is therefore correct for `frameLength ≥ 1440` and **too narrow
for `frameLength = 720`**: the 40-piece probe contains a conforming span (id 36, 1-beat
frame, intensity 1.07) whose *entire* warp is **4.03 ms** — below the floor, while still
costing the full 13 tokens. Normative: use the deadband for the sampler's **fastest**
tempo, i.e. `[0.91, 1.10]` at `frameLength = 720` with the current `[40, 200]` bpm range,
`[0.89, 1.12]` if v3 widens the range to 240 bpm.

**Measured (findings §B, 40 meico-rendered pieces).** The true explanation is a
13-token rubato span at **≤5e-13 ms**. Tempo-only rivals: the finest
*canonical-density* staircase (4-beat grid) leaves **34.0 ms** median; the optimal
**beat-aligned** staircase — a family 4× denser than canonical tempo is allowed to be —
still leaves **8.4 ms** and costs 145 tokens; reaching 10 ms with sub-beat boundaries takes
a median of **11 instructions / 124 tokens = 5.85× the DL** of the truth. Rubato is not a
notational convenience; it is a genuinely cheaper hypothesis class. The 34.0 ms is a
**median, not a bound**: 12/40 pieces fall below 28 ms and 4/40 below 10 ms on the 4-beat
grid (min 2.6 ms), all four with intensity just outside the deadband (0.84–1.15). For
those the tempo/rubato split is *not* cleanly separated — which is what the widened R3
deadband above is for.

### A — articulationMap

| id | rule | why the inverse is well-posed |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | articulation attaches to ~**15 % of distinct onset dates** (Bernoulli per date) | the other 85 % of dates are *clean observations of the dynamics and tempo curves*. If articulation were dense, the velocity field and the curve would be jointly unidentifiable (every note could be explained by its own articulation). 15 % keeps "no articulation" the majority class and leaves ≥5 clean dates per 4-beat segment at the current note density (median 51.5 notes / 16–48 beats) |
| **A2** | `relativeDuration ∈ [0.40, 1.15]`, 2 decimals, **excluding `[0.97, 1.03]`** | the deadband is the observability floor: a 3 % duration change on a 180-tick note at 120 bpm is ~5 ms, at the edge of what offset timing can resolve; below it the instruction is unfalsifiable but still costs ~13 tokens. The asymmetric range encodes that staccato is common and over-legato is rare |
| **A3** | `absoluteVelocityChange ∈ [−25, 25]`, integer, **excluding `[−2, 2]`** | ±1 is at the resolution of the *emitted MIDI* velocity, so it cannot be distinguished from rounding of the dynamics curve once the performance leaves meico as MIDI. ±2 is the smallest reliably observable step. The ±25 cap keeps the deviation from swamping the curve (curve range is `[30, 115]`). **Caveat**: the observable in our JSONL is *not* quantised — meico writes `velocity` as a continuous float (e.g. `32.111028088559394`), so within this pipeline a ±1 change *is* observable, and the deadband is a deliberate robustness margin toward real MIDI rather than a hard identifiability bound |
| **A4** | articulation is applied per **date**, not per note — all notes of a chord share it | otherwise a chord gives several competing per-note explanations for one observable; per-date articulation is also what `ArticulationMap` addresses natively |
| **A5** | only `relativeDuration` and `absoluteVelocityChange` — **no** `absoluteDelay`, `absoluteDelayMs`, `absoluteDuration*Ms`, `relativeVelocity`, `detune*` | H3. `absoluteDelay` moves onsets (aliases rubato/asynchrony); the `*Ms` variants are applied *after* the tempo map (`Performance.java:549`) and so would be a second, tempo-independent timing channel; `relativeVelocity` and `absoluteVelocityChange` are two spellings of one thing |

**Measured (findings §C).** How far apart, in ms, are `meanTempoAt = 0.30` and `0.70` over a

| L (beats) | ratio 1.05 | 1.11 | 1.25 | 1.50 | 2.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.44 | 0.93 | 1.93 | 3.35 | 5.27 |

## 5. The canonical DSL (v3) and its description length

Grammar (linear, digit-tokenised numbers, dates in **beats**):

```
piece        := tempo* dynamics* rubato* articulation*        (grouped by map, dates ascending)
tempo        := 'T' date 'B' bpm ( 'C' | 'R' to 'M' meanTempoAt )
dynamics     := 'D' date 'V' volume ( 'C' | 'R' to 'Q' curvature 'P' protraction )
rubato       := 'U' date 'F' frameBeats 'I' intensity 'X' endDate
articulation := 'A' date 'L' relativeDuration 'W' velocityChange
```

`T D C R M B V Q P -` and the digits are already in `python/dsl.py::VOCAB`; v3 appends
`U F I X A L W` (7 tokens → vocab 31) *after* the existing entries so token ids stay stable.

`X endDate` is the neutral terminator of R6 — it compiles to a full `<rubato>` element with
`intensity="1" lateStart="0" earlyEnd="1"` and the inherited `frameLength`.
Spelling it as one token instead of a second full instruction saves ~5 tokens per span and,
more importantly, makes the terminator *unforgettable* by the grammar.

The `rubato` production deliberately has **no slot for `lateStart` / `earlyEnd`**: R2 pins
them to 0/1. Anything the sampler emits outside 0/1 is therefore not representable, and
until the R2 conflict above is resolved one span in five is unrepresentable. If the
decision goes the other way, extend the production to

```
rubato := 'U' date 'F' frameBeats 'I' intensity [ 'S' lateStart 'E' earlyEnd ] 'X' endDate
```

(vocab 33, `S`/`E` appended after `U F I X A L W`), which is what
`analysis/mdl.py::dl_rubato_span(..., late_start=, early_end=)` already prices.

Everything else in the XML (`xml:id`, `corresp`, `styleDef`s, `beatLength="0.25"`,
`loop="true"`) is compiler-generated, hence costs zero description length.

Typical costs (median, 100 `val_v2` pieces): tempo map **38** tokens, dynamics map **40**,
whole piece **85.5** tokens for a median 51.5 notes = **1.66 tokens/note**. For scale, a
partitura-style dense codec spends ≥1 *number* per note per attribute.

`analysis/mdl.py` implements this counter and is verified byte-exact against
`python/dsl.py::encode_piece` on all 1000 `val_v2` pieces (0 mismatches).

## 6. Canonicalisation procedure (what a validator must enforce)

A predicted or externally-authored MPM is canonicalised by applying these rewrites to a
fixed point. Each rewrite is **render-preserving**; the procedure must be idempotent.

1. drop instructions after the first at any duplicate date (last wins);
2. sort each map by date; insert a default instruction at date 0 if missing (G3);
3. rewrite degenerate transitions to constants: `to == bpm`; `meanTempoAt <= 0` → constant at
   `to`; `meanTempoAt >= 1` → constant at `bpm`; missing `meanTempoAt` → 0.5 (G7/T3/T4);
4. force the final instruction of every map constant (G7);
5. round every literal to canonical precision (G6);
6. merge adjacent instructions that are now identical (G8);
7. snap tempo/dynamics/rubato dates to the beat grid; reject sub-beat dates (G4);
8. close every rubato span with a neutral terminator, set `loop="true"` (R4/R6);
9. drop articulations inside the deadbands (A2/A3) and any articulation attribute outside
   the A5 whitelist;
10. re-check the minimum segment lengths (T1/D1) and merge violating segments;
11. enforce R8: for every rubato span, either raise `frameLength` to a divisor of the
    offsets of all interior tempo dates, or split the span at the offending tempo dates.
    A map violating R8 is **not** canonicalisable by rounding — its render is
    order-dependent and may contain NaN — so this step may reject.

**Acceptance test for the canonicaliser**: `render(canonicalise(M)) == render(M)` to
≤1e-9 ms and ≤0 velocity units, and `canonicalise(canonicalise(M)) == canonicalise(M)`
token-for-token.

## 7. Known residual ill-posedness (v3 does not fix these)

1. **First-boundary tempo of a concave transition.** With `meanTempoAt < 0.5` the tempo at
   `x = 0` is barely observable from beat-level IOIs (already noted in the project memory:
   ~10 BPM error on strongly non-linear curves). The normal form does not remove this; it is
   an *estimation* limit, not an aliasing one. Mitigation: keep `meanTempoAt >= 0.15`.
2. **Rubato frame = 4 beats vs a 4-beat tempo transition.** At the largest allowed frame the
   warp is 94 % absorbable by a beat-aligned tempo map (findings §B). The rules keep the
   *canonical* rival (4-beat staircase, 67.6 ms median in that subgroup) far away, but a
   hybrid decoder that is allowed sub-beat tempo boundaries could still trade them. If a
   residual ambiguity shows up in training, drop 2880 from R1. Note the beat-aligned floor
   used for the "94 %" is the optimum over beat-aligned *piecewise-constant* maps, not over
   all beat-aligned maps (a power transition can bend within a segment): it is a valid
   bracket, not a proven universal lower bound.
3. **Articulation velocity vs a fast dynamics transition.** A sequence of same-signed
   `absoluteVelocityChange` values on consecutive dates is an unrolled crescendo. A1's 15 %
   density makes long runs improbable but does not forbid them; a "no ≥3 consecutive
   same-signed articulations" rule would close it.
4. **Chords.** All notes at a date share tempo, dynamics, rubato and articulation, so a chord
   contributes one observation, not `k`. Piece-level statistics should weight by *distinct
   onset dates*, not notes.
5. **Out of scope, deliberately**: asynchrony, imprecision, movement/pedal, metrical
   accentuation, ornamentation. Each needs its own band before it can be sampled (§1).
6. **meico's rubato/tempo composition is key-based, not date-based** (see R8). Even under
   R8 the two maps compose asymmetrically: note *onsets* take their tempo segment from the
   unwarped key, whereas note *offsets* go through `pendingDurations` and take theirs from
   the already-warped `date.end.perf` (`TempoMap.java:422-436`, `RubatoMap.java:386-397`).
   R8 makes the onset path well-defined; nothing in v3 depends on the offset path, and the
   Python model in `analysis/identifiability.py` deliberately covers onsets only. Any
   future work that fits articulation `relativeDuration` against rendered *offsets* under
   an active rubato span must re-derive that path first.
7. **Floating point.** The exactness proofs bottom out at ~1.5e-11 ms, not 0: Java's
   `Math.pow` (fdlibm) and CPython's libm differ by 1 ULP on the same arguments (verified
   bit-for-bit, `0x1.c49f151d727fap-1` vs `...f9p-1`). With meico's own warped dates fed in
   and no power transitions in play, the Python composition is bit-exact (proof V5,
   0.000000000000 ms). Every "0.0 ms" in earlier releases of this document was a `%.9f`
   formatting artifact over residuals of this size.
